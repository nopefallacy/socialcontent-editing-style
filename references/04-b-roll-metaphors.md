## 1. What the user means by good B-roll

In their words: *"intentful and visually flowing"*, *"highlighting what matters and how to showcase it"*,
*"a very crafted work, not some random bullshit"*, *"native to the editing"*, *"makes sense at a glance and is
amazing to look at"*, *"every B-roll must evoke a feeling, carry a metaphor and a small visual story, and have
satisfying, unique motion — execution and taste over complexity"*.

Translate that into a working definition:

> A B-roll is a small **interface** (from the user's reference set) that **changes state on the spoken word**,
> showing **cause → effect**, built from **real material** where possible, **highlighting one thing** while the
> rest steps back — and it is a **piece of the same system** as every other B-roll in the video.

## 2. Step 1 — one throughline system per video

Before any single beat, choose the one system the whole video lives in. The video's argument becomes a sequence
of state changes inside it. This is what makes the B-roll "flow": the viewer learns the system once, and every
later beat is that system moving forward.

How to choose:
0. **Listen for the speaker's own image first.** People explain with pictures: "speak to it as a friend",
   "you cannot magically solve it with someone else's insight", "a perfect script", "it ripples". If the speaker
   gives you an image — especially one they repeat — the throughline is built from it (video 6: the speaker
   said "friend" eight times → "the lens is a friend on a call": a REC button becomes a video call whose friend
   tile turns on and nods). Their image always beats a generic one.
1. Write the message in one sentence and the **turn** (the belief it flips).
   *v7: "A perfect script still sounds unnatural — content is communication, not words; write it, speak it,
   re-record, keep a few anchor sentences."*
2. Ask: **what familiar interface could hold the whole argument — the problem, the why, the fix, the what-next
   — as states?** Candidates:
   - a **workflow** of steps (pending → running → done / failed / skipped) — process and method videos (v7)
   - a **video call** (tiles on/off, a friend nodding, mute, reactions) — speaking/presence videos (v6: "the lens
     is a friend on a call")
   - an **editor timeline** (clips, playhead, cuts, pins, speed) — content-making videos (v1, v6 review strip)
   - a **switchboard / integrations** (sources → toggles → output) — "it's more than X" videos (v7 scene)
   - a **docs tree / sidebar** with a travelling marker — "what this channel is about", structure videos (v4)
   - a **chat / AI prompt** card with a cursor — tools and AI videos
   - a **milestone slider** (struck → current → next) — growth, numbers, progress (v4)
   - a **physical system** when the idea is physical: a drop and its ripples on a surface (v5: repetition beats
     luck), a production line with a jam (v3: the bottleneck)
3. It must: be recognisable in one glance; have states that can change on a word; host real material (frames of
   the speaker, their footage, their numbers, their script); fit the reference grammar (§5).
4. Write it at the top of `BRIEF.md` as "The throughline". Every beat in the table names which part of the
   system it uses.
5. **Kit pieces are the alphabet, not the system.** Steps, labels, toggles, cursors and photo nodes are reused
   in every video; the *system* they build (a workflow, a call, an editor, a switchboard) should change with the
   content. Don't rebuild the previous video's system unless this video is genuinely about the same thing (a
   direct sequel or the same process) — and say so in your report.

## 3. Step 2 — the eight questions for every beat

Answer these in order and write the answers into the beat table. If you can't answer 1–4 crisply, the beat is
captions-only.

1. **What is the verb?** Reduce the sentence to a state change: add, check off, fail, skip, replace, pin a
   problem, compare, select part of a whole, connect, deliver, turn on/off, fill, count up, slow down, clear,
   hand off, wait/pending.
2. **Which object in the system performs that verb?** (a step, a label, a toggle, a selection box, a
   connector, a counter, a live window, a cursor). Prefer an object already on screen.
3. **On which spoken word does the change happen?** Take the word's time from the transcript. The state change
   lands *on* the word, not before, not a sentence later.
4. **What is the ONE thing highlighted?** And what dims (opacity ~0.35), blurs (1.5px) or gets struck? Focus by
   dimming, not by adding arrows.
5. **What real material can be in it?** A frame of the speaker's hands when they say "hand gestures"; their real
   pauses; their face as the "you" node; a live window of the take for "watch it back"; this video's own script
   for "your script"; their real numbers. Real beats invented every time.
6. **Where does it live?** (§4) captions only · a card beside the face · a rack-focus scene · a hero word behind
   the head.
7. **Where did it come from, where does it go?** Is this the same card returning? Does it hand off to the next
   beat (a dive into a node, a chip that reappears later)? Is there a callback to an earlier state?
8. **Does it read with the sound off, in two seconds?** If a viewer can't tell what changed and why, simplify.

## 4. Step 3 — where each beat lives

| use | when | how long | budget (per ~2 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **captions only** | the line is personal, transitional, or already clear | — | ≥ 25% of runtime: breathing room is part of the flow |
| **card beside the face** (screen-locked, on the caption side) | a step, a state, a small system change | 4–18s, can return | 3–5 appearances of the same 1–2 cards |
| **rack-focus scene** | the idea needs the whole frame: an inside view (watch your take), a many-part system (switchboard), a method demo (read → look up → speak) | 6–10s | 2–3 |
| **hero word behind the head** | the emotional word of a beat | 0.8–1.2s | 3 |

Rhythm: footage-led openings (the face and the hook first), the first rack scene around 25–35% in, the method /
payoff scene in the last third, end on the face (with a forward-pointing card only if the speaker points forward).

Which lines get a visual: **the conceptual core** — the lines that carry the argument, especially abstract ones
("there is no friend nodding along", "the opposite side of the perspective") — are exactly where a metaphor
helps most; don't leave them captions-only because they are abstract. Captions-only is for personal asides,
transitions, repeats and the breath after a big beat. If a line can only get a visual by inventing filler,
the litmus tests (§9) win over the budget.

Claims about the footage: if a visual says what the speaker does on camera ("looking down", "hands"), check
the frames at those times first, or word it as a checklist rather than a moment.

## 7. Worked example: video 7, beat by beat (approved)

Message: a perfect script still sounds unnatural. Throughline: **one workflow, from script to speaking.**

What the first version got wrong (rejected): a different gadget for every line (a multitrack editor scene, a
stacked bar, a record card, a teleprompter), opaque white cards, dark/paper stages cutting away from the
speaker, tiny text, and an "anchors" beat that showed "66 words → 3 sentences" instead of the actual technique
(read one sentence → look up → say it → next). **Listen to how the speaker explains the idea and show that
mechanism, not a statistic about it.**

## 8. Continuity: carriers, callbacks, growing cards

- **Same card, same place:** a card that returns appears where it was, with the state it left in.
- **Dive hand-off:** before a rack scene, the on-footage card scales toward the centre and blurs out while the
  scene's card grows from that spot; after the scene, its result is carried back onto the footage in a small card.
- **Callbacks:** problems pinned in a scene return as labels in the fix and get cleared by the step that fixes
  them. Plan 1–2 per video.
- **Grow, don't pre-allocate:** a card is only as tall as its visible content; it grows (0.55s expo.out) when a
  step or result is added.
- **Hand-written human details** where they mean something: cursors named "you"/"me", "skip it", "don't need
  it", "eyes on the page", "look up · say it".

## 9. Litmus tests before you build

For every visual beat, all must be "yes":
1. Can you say its metaphor and its cause → effect in one sentence?
2. Does a state change land on a specific spoken word?
3. Is it part of the video's one system (or a deliberate, related scene of it)?
4. Is the highlighted thing exactly what is being said right now?
5. Is real material used wherever the line refers to something real?
6. Will every text in it be ≥ 27px (UI) / ≥ 58px (captions) in the 1440 world?
7. Is it translucent glass on the footage (or a rack scene), not an opaque white box or a separate stage?
8. Does it stay off the face for its whole life (check the shot's end frame)?
9. Would the user call it random, a chart for a feeling, or "for the sake of B-roll"? If maybe — rethink.

## 10. Anti-patterns (each one was rejected by the user)

| rejected | verdict | do instead |
|---|---|---|
| emoji, cartoons, sky-blue template B-roll | "childish" | refined UI pieces from the refs |
| gradients, strokes, bloom, fisheye, purple auras | "flashy, unprofessional" | flat or frosted, one accent |
| a bar chart for "this hard" / the bottleneck | "lazy", "low quality" | a living system that acts it out (timeline that collapses, production line) |
| abstract props (a lever, a bento of bars) | "confusing" | a real interface or the real thing |
| invented fake ads / placeholder UIs | "AI-ish" | the user's real clips, posts, frames |
| a pond surface, a fingerprint print with no interaction | "for the sake of adding", "no concept" | an interface metaphor that acts the sentence |
| a new gadget per sentence (v7 v3) | "random", "not native", "not visually flowing" | one throughline system |
| near-opaque white cards | "you just made it proper white — it was translucent" | frosted glass |
| small grey uppercase labels, thin serif titles | "typography doesn't suit" | caption-family headlines + one serif word |
| tiny clip labels and pins | "text is not properly visible" | ≥ 31px UI text |
